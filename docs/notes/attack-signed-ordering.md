# Attack pass on design-signed-ordering.md

Written break-it pass by a non-author (claude-7c09c4) on [design-signed-ordering.md](../design-signed-ordering.md) at `4df9fedef`
(`origin/0.3dev`), read against `app/poolhouse-node/src` (`rules.rs`, `row.rs`, `board.rs`, `log.rs`, `sync.rs`,
`lease/merge.rs`, `lease/table.rs`, `tasks/fold.rs`, `gov/roster.rs`, `gov/chain.rs`, `gov/pool.rs`, `gov/clock.rs`). Nothing was run;
every finding is from reading code and design. Decisions already taken by the lead: quorum 2 of 2 in a pool of two; fail closed in a
pool of three or more with a majority away. Words follow [glossary.md](../glossary.md).

Verdict: the causal rule (rule 1) is sound and ends S1 and S2 for an honest-but-wrong writer. Rule 2 (quorum time) as written does not
hold against a minority of liars, and four spec gaps (N1 to N4 below) let a single member or a sleeping device defeat it or the
governance holds. Fixes are proposed for each; one replacement of rule 2 (N1) closes most of them.

## 1. Findings

Severity: **H** breaks a claim the design makes, **M** a gap that needs a decision before the slice, **L** a note.

| # | Sev | Case | Attack | Hole or ok | Proposed fix |

| N1 | H | Liar pair in a 5-member pool; receipts forge quorum time; compromised or malicious member | `at` has no lower bound: `check_chain` only refuses a wall that falls below the writer's previous entry, so a liar writes `at = 1` in its first entry and never advances it. Its receipts, and the author's own `at` in 3.4, are free votes at time 1 for its own claim X' and withheld votes (infinity) for the victim's X. In n = 5, k = 3 with 2 liars: q(X') = time of the first honest cite of X'; q(X) = time of the third honest cite of X, that is the last honest member (3 honest, 2 liars at infinity). X' made after seeing X wins whenever it reaches one honest member before the last honest member has cited X; one honest device asleep for a day makes the window a day. Same in n = 3 (1 liar): X' needs one honest cite, X needs both honest. The design says "fewer than k liars cannot place an entry before the first honest member"; that is true and is all the liar needs. Tests in slice 3 ("withhold-and-release loses", "k - 1 liars cannot place before the first honest member") test the slow variant, not observe-and-race. | **Hole.** A majority quorum tolerates crashes, not liars: two quorums of size k meet in 2k - n members, 1 in n = 5, and that one can be the liar. | Replace "time of the k-th cite" by a vote by position in each member's own chain: member M votes for the candidate whose cite (or own entry) comes first in M's log; a cite of two candidates in one entry, or of neither, is no vote. The winner is the plurality of votes, ties by seniority (N6). A liar casts one vote; clocks and `at` are not read, so skew and low `at` stop mattering (this also removes the two-clock comparison in a pool of two, where the device with the slower clock currently decides an honest contest). To beat X the liars now need honest members that held X' before X, not one cite. State the tolerance: with k = floor(n/2) + 1 it is crash-only; for liars use k = ceil((n + f + 1) / 2), f = floor((n - 1) / 3) (n = 4: 3, 5: 4, 7: 5), and say a pool of three has no tolerance of a liar. Add slice-3 tests for observe-and-race with 1 liar of 3 and 2 of 5, one honest device asleep. |
| N2 | H | Quorum size on a board | 3.4 says n is "the number of origins on the board whose writer is an active member"; 3.4 and 5.1 elsewhere say k comes from the roster. An origin exists only once a device has written on that board (`Board::origin_of`, `logs` built from files). On a fresh or quiet board, one member that writes first is n = 1, k = 1: it is its own quorum, and a second writer makes n = 2, k = 2 with 3 members of 5 absent. | **Hole** (text conflict with a real consequence). | n and k come from the roster's active members, never from origins present; a member without an origin on the board has no vote (it counts toward n, not toward reached). Write it once in 3.4 and in 4.2. |
| N3 | H | Sybil admission at 5 an hour reaching quorum; open pool | Open pool, 5 honest members, one hostile member. Admits are 5 an hour per member, 10 an hour for the pool (`OPEN_PER_HOUR`, `OPEN_POOL_PER_HOUR`), each sybil admitted counts at once (q of the admit is finite because honest devices receipt it). n = 5 + s, k = floor(n/2) + 1: s = 6 gives 6 of 11, a majority, in under an hour at 10 an hour. The sybils then own q (N1), pool time (4.5) and receipts: a removal, cancel or veto act never reaches k receipts, so no hold ever starts (N4): the person cannot remove the sybils; only a new pool recovers. 8 accepts "the reach of the malicious-member case"; it is a total takeover plus a governance lock, not one member's reach. | **Hole.** | A member's vote counts only after its admit has stood for a period (the delegate window, hours to days) and is by admit order, so a burst of new members adds no votes to any contest or hold that began before them; roster-as-of (N5) gives the same effect for contests already open. Restrictive acts must not wait for receipts (N4). |
| N4 | H | A hold that fails closed used as denial of governance; stolen device key; takeover | Removal of a stolen device needs `q(act)`, the k-th receipt, to start its hold (4.5), and 4.5 stalls holds when no fresh majority exists. Pool of 3: laptop stolen, one honest device asleep: n = 3, k = 2, the thief writes no receipt, one honest receipt is not k: the removal never starts and the thief stays a member. Today one device's awake clock suffices. Any withholding majority, a sleeping majority, or N3 sybils lock all governance. Also the cancel window "q(cancel) below q(act) plus hold": liars vote early for the attacker's act and late for the cancel (N1), so a cancel is out of time while an honest member sleeps longer than the hold. "Fail closed" is fail open for a restriction: the removed device keeps its standing on every device that has not seen a quorum. | **Hole.** The two decisions leave the person with no way to remove a dead or hostile device while the majority is away, against "removing the device is a person's choice and never a wait" (3.8). | Treat acts by direction. A restriction (remove, revoke, leave, veto, a charter that narrows) takes effect when its hold has run on the device's awake clock from first sight AND, when a quorum is reachable, the record agrees; it never waits for receipts, and the subject of a removal is not counted in n. A permission (admit, delegate, widen) waits for the quorum. Pool time is `max(record, awake)` for "never early" on both measures; the cap that a fast honest clock (a day ahead) can set pool time via the k-th largest `at` is closed by the awake bound. `status` names the devices a stalled hold is waiting on so the person can remove them. This also settles open question 2 for restrictions. |
| N5 | H | Roster-dependent k; sybil and admit churn; circular standing | q and k are read against the roster, and the roster is folded from acts whose standing (4.4, the rate) reads q. For one new admit Z: with Z in n the k grows and the admit's own q can become infinity; without Z it is finite: no fixed point is defined. Separately a settled claim is not final: adding members raises k and each new member's first cite covers the old claim at the member's join time (cites cover a prefix), so a claim that was settled by k is re-ranked later, and 10 sybils that omit cites for X and cite their own X' (N3) unseat the holder retroactively. | **Hole** (undefined order plus retroactive reorder). | Evaluate each decision against the roster as of the entry: the members whose admit ranks before the entry by rules 1 and 3 (no q), walked in dn order, each admit judged against the roster before it. A claim with every member of that roster having voted is final: a later entry from a later member cannot displace it. Write the evaluation order in 4.4. |
| N6 | H | Grinding the identity hash; S1 at level 1; open pool | Rule 3 is a fixed total order over identities. A member that omits cites makes every contest concurrent, so at level 1 (no receipts) the lowest hash wins every contest it enters, always, not "one time in two" (7 and 2 table). The hash is of pool id and key, chosen at keygen: a device grinds 2^32 keys offline in hours, joins an open pool with a near-zero hash, and wins every tie for good. In a closed pool the person adds the key without seeing its hash. The slice-2 test "S1 as a test with a hand-built log wins nothing" is a pass for a member whose hash is merely higher. | **Hole** in open pools (accepted risk text is wrong: no person adds the key); closed pools are ok when the accepted-risk line is kept. | Break ties by seniority: the rank of the member's admit act (the founding device first), which a joiner cannot choose, in place of the key hash. Keep the hash only as the last tie among equal admits. Add a slice-2 test with a hand-ground low hash. |
| N7 | H | Receipt flood; log fill; malicious writer; agent | A hostile writer produces ordered entries (acquires, tasks) at its quota rate; every honest device writes one receipt per sync round in which it stored one. A round is not rate-limited, so each honest log grows by a receipt per round for as long as the writer keeps writing, per board. The receipts are `Class::Node` in `logbounds`, held "to the whole log less the last stretch", a smaller reserve than an author's, so they can fill the log until the device's own claims return `Quota`. One writer thus starves the claims of every other member. | **Hole.** | At most one receipt per board per minute, coalesced; receipts only for contested kinds (an open acquire of a pool-wide key, a task claim), not for releases, ends or task creation; a share cap (for example 10 percent of the log) so receipts never use an author's reserve; receipts older than a base entry are dropped at rotation. Bound the index: the cite index is entry cap times cite cap per origin, times up to 64 origins per board (`MAX_ORIGINS`), about 200 million lines at the stated caps; state the board-wide bound the test checks. |
| N8 | H | Base-entry `carry` taken on the writer's word; joiner told a false carry | A writer W rotates and writes `carry` entries for settled pool-wide claims with an empty `beats` list. A device J that joins later never held the old segment, so it cannot check `of` or `beats` (5.2 item 4). J ranks W's carry as settled; every device that held the old segment checks the base, finds the lie and marks W damaged, but "damaged" is a per-device flag in `state.json`: there is no proof J can fetch, and old members stop serving W's origin, so J never hears. J and the pool disagree for good. In a pool of two, J has only the writer. Separately, 5.2 item 3 writes a base only when every active member has the claims, and item 4 keeps the old segment until every active member has acknowledged it: one dead or hostile member stops rotation forever and the log fills to its 200,000 cap. | **Hole** (false carry unprovable by a joiner; rotation liveness). | A carry counts for a joiner only when the base entry is cited by k members of the roster as of the base (their cites are the check, made by devices that held the old segment; a device cites nothing of an origin it holds as damaged, which a test pins). Until then it is unreached and falls to the seniority tie. Rotate when k members have acknowledged; the others are treated as joiners. |
| N9 | M | `dn` counter reused after restore from backup, clone or VM snapshot | `dn` is a file written before signing, so a restored state directory rolls it back and the device signs a second act with an old `dn`. Two acts with one (device, `dn`) are a fork and "both are void and that device signs no delegate admit" (4.1): the already-effective first act is voided. If "signs no delegate admit" applies backwards, every device that delegate admitted loses standing. The same restore forks the board log (same origin, old `seq`): `Board::store` returns `Damaged` and marks the origin damaged for good, and a fresh origin is refused by `peer_agrees` with `DIFFERENT_LOG`, so today's code already has no way back; slice 4 would cut the origin on every device. A thief of a delegate key forks on purpose to void its acts. Gaps in `dn` are harmless (only equality is a fork). | **Hole** (no recovery path; retroactivity undefined). | On opening, the device raises its `dn` to one above the highest `dn` of its own acts in any act it holds, and does not sign until its first sync has finished; a device that finds a peer holds its origin past its own tip (or with another hash) starts a fresh origin, and peers accept a new origin for a fingerprint when the old one is cut at a fork. Say the fork voids the two acts and later acts of that device from the fork's rank, never earlier ones. |
| N10 | M | Cites: a hostile writer citing nothing, citing everything, or a bad cite; cite-vector abuse up to 64 | Nothing: omitting only gives up rule 1 (the writer then looks concurrent and, with N1 fixed, has one vote). Everything: cites cover a prefix and only rise, so a writer that cites all ranks after all and loses its own contests; it cannot move another entry. Left: (a) 3.1 caps a cite list at 16 and does not say which 16 when more origins moved; an honest claim in a pool with more than 16 active origins can omit a rival it held and flap; `MAX_ORIGINS` is 64. (b) "A cite to an entry the reader does not hold yet waits" is undefined: a cite of a seq beyond the origin's tip, kept forever, is an unbounded pending entry. (c) A body that says it is a base is the only way to 64 cites; if "base" is a field the writer sets, any writer gets 64 cites and the `carry` semantics. (d) A cite with a bad hash "counts for nothing" but still raises the writer's "cites only rise" floor, so the writer's own later honest cites are refused (self-harm only). | **Ok** for omit and cite-everything; **gap** for (a) to (c). | (a) The 16 are the origins with an open competing claim first, then the rest by recency; write it. (b) An unresolved cite is no cite after one round and the entry stands; cap pending at a small number per origin. (c) A base is recognised only by being the first entry after a rotation the writer's `seq` and `prev` carry (5.2 item 1), never by a body field. |
| N11 | M | A hold that fails closed, pool time, Windows sleep | Pool time is the k-th largest newest `at`; liars give huge values, so with k - 1 liars pool time equals the largest honest `at`; one honest device a day fast makes every hold end a day early (the `held_back` five minutes apply only if the reader reads its own clock, which 4.5 says it does not). Readers also stall when fewer than k members wrote in the last hour. | **Gap**; "no device's own clock is read" is also not true if held-back entries are dropped. | `max(record, awake)` as in N4; apply the five-minute hold-back to entries entering pool time and say it reads the local clock. |
| N12 | M | Two-device pools: quorum 2 of 2 (lead's decision) | With n = k = 2, a hostile partner writes no receipt for your claim and a receipt-free claim after seeing yours: q(X) = infinity, q(X') finite once your device cites it (it does so automatically), so the partner wins every contest it chooses to enter, however late. That is today's S1 for pools of two, not closed. With honest partners the winner is whichever device's clock reads earlier at its own cite, so skew decides. | **Hole**, accepted by decision; the cost is not stated in 8. | The vote-by-position rule of N1 gives a tie in a pool of two (one vote each), settled by seniority, with no receipts to withhold and no clock to read; it is the same 2-of-2 rule with the exploit removed. If the lead keeps time-based q, add the sentence "in a pool of two a partner that omits cites wins every contest it enters" to 8. |

Effect is limited to leave against admit and rank among acts, since admin-set forks use signed data only. | **Ok**, low; note it as accepted: a person signs bytes the sentence does not cover. | Name it in 8. |
| N14 | L | Eclipse of a joiner | A host that hands a joiner the `pool` board can omit a removal act and the receipts of its quorum: the joiner's roster shows the removed device as active; permissive acts fail closed (unreached) but a restriction it never saw simply is not applied. ## 2. The AGENTS.md section 1 cases

| Case | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Copied or cloned pool or device | Clone = restore = fork: the origin and `dn` handling has no recovery path (N9); a copied pool is refused by pool id (ok). |
| Replay | Ok: board in the hash, pool in the head, `idem` per origin, act names its pool and nonce. One check to add: a replayed receipt is the same entry (same hash), so it cannot count twice; a test pins it. |
| Forged or below-threshold signature | Ok for forgery (cites sit under the head signature). Below threshold: k from the roster (N2), and a liar's votes are valid signatures with false times (N1). |
| Malicious member | N1, N5, N7. |
| Stolen device key | N4 (removal can stall), N9 (forks as denial of the owner's log), N1 (post-removal entries race the removal's q, 4.6, in the same window). |
| Stolen phone or person key | Ok; no order reads a person signature; see N13. |
| Compromised or injected agent | Ok: the node writes cites and receipts; text decides no rank. A body cannot mark itself a base (N10c). |
| Downgrade | Ok for `VERSION` and act version; one addition: the quorum flavour must not be a flag (N1 states k is derived). |
| Approval fatigue | No new prompt. A hold that stalls (N4, N11) shows the person no reason and no action; `status` must name the devices it waits on. |
| Takeover by whoever is present | Ok: cannot change an order, a cite or a vote; can sign that device's own `leave` and delegate admits as today. |
| Eclipse of one device during a hold | N4 (fail closed is fail open for a removal), N14. |
| Sybil admission | N3, N5, N6. |
| Withholding then releasing | Ok on the slow variant (loses); the observe-and-race variant wins (N1). |

## 3. Are S1 to S6 closed

| Seq | Closed? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 backdated claim | **No** for a member that omits cites and has the lowest identity hash, ground or not (N6), or is the partner in a pool of two (N12), or controls f votes (N1). Closed for the plain backdating of `hlc`. |
| S2 clock only wrong | Yes: `at` orders nothing, and dropping `observe` removes the drag-forward by a row dated up to five minutes ahead. |
| S3 delivery order of admits | Yes for delivery order, once N5 fixes the evaluation order; the rate is then over a roster the same on every device. A liar still picks which of its own admits stand, as it picks what to sign. |
| S4 leave against admit | Yes when the leave is reached; an unreached leave ranks behind an admit (a device that left while most members slept is re-admitted by a delegate: the device's own wish is lost, low). |
| S5 hold kept from one device | For validity yes (record). For effect the stall is fail open for restrictions (N4). |
| S6 the joiner's word | Yes for the host's word on holds and rate; not for the host's choice of acts (N14); carry is still the writer's word (N8). |

## 4. What is ok as written

- Rule 1 (causal): a claim cannot rank ahead of what it cites; the node writes cites, bodies cannot.
- Dropping `hlc.observe` and `tick`: removes S2's ratchet.
- Cites inside the entry hash and covered by the head: a relay cannot add, drop or change one.
- A member that never writes receipts breaks nothing for others.
- Fork proof (slice 4) as a mechanism, given N9's recovery path.
- Acts' `at_ms` removed from every order: closes S4.
